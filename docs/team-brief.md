# Team Brief: No Fate But What We Make

## What this is

You know the drill with AI tooling sessions. Someone books a lunch-and-learn, shows some slides about prompts, everyone nods politely, and three weeks later precisely nothing has changed except the calendar has one more recording nobody watches. I've sat through those. You've sat through those. We're not doing that.

Instead, I'm going to show you my actual day. Live. Agents grinding away unattended while I do other things, one command that restores my entire working world, six client tenants juggled without a single auth tantrum, and timesheets that fill themselves in (yes, really, and yes, that segment alone is worth showing up for). Nothing gets described that isn't demonstrated, and nothing gets demonstrated that I don't genuinely do every day. No hypotheticals, no "imagine if", no slideware.

The clever bit isn't any one tool. It's that the whole program is built as a system for actually adopting this stuff, not a one-off moment of inspiration that evaporates by Friday.

## Why three stages (there's method in it, promise)

Think of it as a funnel: **ignite, immerse, embed**. Each stage exists because of what the previous one creates, and the gaps between them are doing work.

**Stage 1: the flagship (60 min).** One hour, tightly packed, everyone comes. The job of this session is NOT to teach you the tooling. It's to make you *want* the tooling, hand you a 30-minute on-ramp for that same evening, and get questions bubbling. One hour also means we all share the same reference point, which matters for everything after.

**Stage 2: Judgement Day (half-day stream, 3 weeks later).** The three-week gap is deliberate, not me being lazy. In that time you'll do the Day One homework, hit real friction, and develop real questions instead of hypothetical ones. The kill list catches them, you vote, and by stream day the agenda is literally whatever the team demanded. Drop-in blocks mean the grads come at 10, the veterans come at 11, and nobody sits through an hour that isn't for them. And it's live and unrehearsed on purpose: the flagship builds excitement, Judgement Day builds trust, because you'll watch things break and get fixed in real time. Nobody believes a demo that never breaks. I certainly don't.

**Stage 3: TryNet (fortnightly, forever).** Here's the truth about every failed tooling rollout ever: adoption dies the moment there's nowhere to ask a dumb question. So TryNet is deliberately tiny. Thirty minutes, no agenda, no prep, lurking encouraged. The barrier to showing up has to be lower than the barrier to asking. The big events create the spike; TryNet is what turns the spike into a habit. Like Skynet, but you try things and nobody gets terminated.

## How the sessions are built (steal these tricks)

A few structural gags are doing the heavy lifting, and honestly we should be nicking them for client enablement too:

- **The salvo arc.** I fire off three real agent tasks at minute 5 and we harvest the results at minute 50. You watch three jobs complete themselves while I was busy doing other things. The session structure literally proves the main claim (delegate first, type second) so you don't have to take my word for anything.
- **Every segment ends twice.** Once for the new folks ("the one thing to take away is X") and once for the veterans ("go poke at Y"). Ten seconds of effort, and nobody in the room is ever the wrong audience. Cheapest trick in the whole pack. Steal it.
- **Show, never describe.** Every tool gets 90 seconds of doing something real or it gets cut. There is exactly ONE slide in the entire program, and it's designed to be screenshotted.
- **Determinism around the chaos.** Flaky demos are pre-recorded, every live demo has a break-glass branch, every segment has a hard time box. Which, you'll notice, is exactly the principle the content teaches: make the environment deterministic so the non-deterministic bits can be trusted. The medium is the message, etc.
- **Nothing dies, it just gets rescheduled.** Anything cut for time lands on the kill list as a vote-able option. You prioritise the backlog, not me.

## Why Velrada should be frothing about this

- **Hours back, immediately.** The Kimble evidence-pack pipeline alone returns real hours per consultant per week, and it needs zero terminal skills. Broadest-reach segment in the whole show.
- **Grads ramp in days, not weeks.** Day One tier plus a fortnightly place to ask questions compresses "productive with agentic tooling" into one evening. Faster ramp is direct margin on every graduate we hire.
- **We literally sell this.** AI enablement is the business. Running a genuinely great internal program is eating our own cooking, and the recording, the repo, and the session design itself become client-facing proof. "Here's how we did it ourselves" beats any deck we've ever shipped.
- **The IP flywheel.** Every Mad Skills PR turns someone's private workflow trick into versioned, shared delivery IP. Each contribution makes the next engagement faster. Compound interest, but for skills.
- **Guardrails included.** Review discipline ("treat agent output like a grad's PR") and data hygiene are baked into the same hour as the tools. You learn the power and the seatbelt together.
- **People stay where the tooling is good.** Cheap culture-building, with a paper trail.

## Phases

**Phase 0: Launch (T-3 weeks)**
- Announcement out, flagship booked, recording on
- Kill list opens: nominate anything real to be built, fixed, or torn apart live
- Repo skeleton created, demo assets staged

**Phase 1: Build and rehearse (T-2 to T-1 weeks)**
- Two full dry runs; the second with a live guinea pig from this team (volunteer wanted, snacks provided)
- Leak hunt on both recordings so no client data ever touches the screen
- Pre-records cut (Kimble pipeline, voice demo, bootstrap timelapse)
- Goodie bag finished and flipped internal-visible; teasers posted

**Phase 2: Flagship (T-0)**
- 60 minutes, seven segments, hard time boxes, one slide
- Agents fired minute 5, harvested minute 50
- Chat wingman needed on the day (volunteer wanted)

**Phase 3: Conversion (T+1 day to T+2 weeks)**
- Chaptered recording, follow-up post, promo clips
- Your homework: Day One tier, 30 minutes, that night. That's it. Don't be a hero and install everything.
- Kill list triaged; Judgement Day winners locked and pre-staged

**Phase 4: Judgement Day (T+3 weeks)**
- 10:00-15:00, drop-in blocks: Genisys (setup from zero), Skynet Core (deep plumbing), Rise of the Machines (lunch lurk, genuinely soothing), The Kill List, Dark Fate (bring your broken thing), Hasta La Vista (wrap)
- Live, unrehearsed, honest debugging; chat wrangler needed (volunteer wanted)

**Phase 5: TryNet forever (ongoing)**
- Fortnightly, 30 min, invite lands the day after the flagship
- No agenda, no prep. Setup problems, "is this a good use case", skill ideas, war stories, lurking. If nobody shows I'll be building something on screen anyway.
- Standing hooks: goodie-bag FAQ additions, Mad Skills PR show-and-tell, new plugin finds
- Honest review at 3 months: if it's not earning its slot, we kill it. No zombie meetings.

## What I need from you lot

1. **Vote the kill list.** I've seeded it, now make it yours. If your problem isn't on there, it doesn't get terminated.
2. **One dry-run guinea pig.** T-1 week, 75 minutes, ideally someone newer to the tooling. Your job is to tell me where you got lost or bored. Brutal honesty appreciated.
3. **One chat wingman** for the flagship and **one chat wrangler** for Judgement Day. I cannot code and moderate. Nobody can.
4. **Bring a broken thing** to Dark Fate. A real problem, your screen, agents unleashed on it together.
5. **PR a skill into Mad Skills.** First contributor gets bragging rights on stream and my eternal respect, which is worth what you'd expect.

## How we'll know it worked

- Day One installs within 48 hours of the flagship
- Kill list submissions and votes before close
- Mad Skills PRs from people who aren't me
- TryNet still has a pulse at week 6, which is when novelty dies and habit either exists or doesn't
